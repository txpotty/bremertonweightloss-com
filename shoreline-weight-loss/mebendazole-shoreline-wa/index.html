    How Mebendazole Treatment Works in Shoreline, WA

    How Mebendazole Works in Shoreline

    Mebendazole blocks a worm's ability to take in glucose by disrupting tiny structures inside its cells called microtubules. The worms starve and die. They pass in the stool over the following days, and most people never notice them, which is normal and does not mean the drug failed. Only a small amount is absorbed into the blood. In the US, it is sold as an FDA-approved chewable tablet.

    Who Mebendazole Is For

    Adults usually ask for it because of pinworm. Nighttime anal itching, especially when kids at home have the same complaint, is the classic sign. It also treats roundworm, whipworm and hookworm, which are more often linked to travel or certain environments. Adults with a positive test qualify. Weight loss and cleanses are not on the list.

    Mebendazole Dosing and Schedule in Shoreline

    For pinworm, the usual plan is a single 100 mg chewable tablet, repeated in two weeks. The repeat dose targets worms that hatched after the first one, since eggs survive treatment. Other intestinal worms are often treated with 100 mg twice daily for three days, followed by a stool test in some cases. Chew it fully. Meals do not matter.

    What to Expect After Taking Mebendazole

    Most people feel nothing after the dose. Some get mild stomach discomfort. Itching usually eases within a few days, and it can take another week for the skin to calm down completely, especially if it was scratched a lot. You may not see worms. Put the two week dose in your phone calendar the day you take the first.

    Side Effects of Mebendazole in Shoreline

    Because so little is absorbed, mebendazole is well tolerated at short courses. Belly pain, loose stools and nausea are the usual complaints. Rash is rare. Extended high-dose use for other purposes can affect blood counts and the liver, so this program uses only standard short courses. Message your provider about a rash, fever or severe stomach pain.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks about symptoms, timing, household members, recent travel and any test results. Pregnancy is a key question. It is usually not given early in pregnancy, and the provider checks breastfeeding status, liver history and current medications before deciding. Taking metronidazole at the same time has been tied to rare but serious skin reactions. Non-pinworm infections usually need a stool test first.

    How Mebendazole Compares to Other Options in Shoreline

    Albendazole works the same way and is sometimes used for the same infections. Over-the-counter pyrantel pamoate is another pinworm option that paralyzes the worms rather than starving them. Ivermectin is preferred for strongyloides, scabies and lice, rather than pinworm. If you are not sure what you have, the antiparasitic protocol helps sort out which medicine fits before anything is prescribed. Your provider recommends the drug with the best evidence.
