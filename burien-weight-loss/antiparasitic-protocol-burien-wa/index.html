    How the Antiparasitic Protocol Works in Burien

    The protocol starts with your symptoms and possible exposures, not with a pill. The provider asks which parasites make sense given where you have traveled, what pets you have and whether people in your household have the same complaints. Testing comes next when it would help. Treatment uses prescription anti-parasitics with established uses, and a follow-up confirms symptoms are gone.

    Who This Protocol Is For

    Adults with symptoms or exposures that raise a fair concern for a parasite are the right fit. The most common case is nighttime anal itching in a home with school-age children, which usually points to pinworm. Others have traveled somewhere intestinal worms are common or already have a positive test from another provider. It is not a detox. It has no role in weight loss either.

    Dosing and Schedule in Burien

    The schedule depends on the drug and the parasite. For pinworm, a single dose of mebendazole 100 mg or albendazole 400 mg is typical, with a second dose two weeks later for newly hatched worms. Ivermectin depends on your body weight. It is usually given as one dose or a short course of one to two days. Your provider writes out exact dates for every dose.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Most courses take one to three days, sometimes with a repeat dose two weeks later. A day or two of mild stomach upset or tiredness after dosing is common. With pinworm, the itching usually eases within a few days, and the remaining irritation can take another week to settle. Week two is the repeat dose. Around week four, your provider may ask for a symptom update or a repeat test.

    Side Effects and Precautions in Burien

    Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea and headache are the side effects people mention most often. With ivermectin, a temporary rash or itch sometimes appears as the parasites die. Mebendazole and albendazole are poorly absorbed into the blood when used for intestinal worms, which keeps most side effects mild at short courses. Pregnancy changes things. Several of these drugs are not used during pregnancy, and the provider will ask.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks when symptoms started, where you have traveled in the past year and whether pets or family members are involved. Prior test results help a lot. Providers also screen for pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver disease and current medications that could interact, such as warfarin with ivermectin. Severe abdominal pain, blood in the stool or a high fever mean in-person care. Some people are asked to finish testing before any medication is prescribed.
